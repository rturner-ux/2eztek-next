Precor makes some of the most durable fitness equipment available, and its treadmills are a fixture in commercial gyms, hotel fitness centers, and high-end home gym setups across the country. A class-action lawsuit was filed alleging that heart rate monitors in Precor treadmills provided inaccurate readings. The complaint alleged that users who relied on those readings to guide their workouts, particularly those training within specific heart rate zones for cardiovascular or weight management goals, received misleading data.

What the Precor Heart Rate Monitor Lawsuit Alleged

The lawsuit targeted the contact-based heart rate sensors embedded in Precor treadmill handlebars. These sensors work by detecting electrical signals through skin contact when a user grips the handles during exercise. The complaint alleged that the readings produced by these sensors were inaccurate to a degree that materially affected how users understood their own cardiovascular exertion during workouts.

Heart rate zone training is a well-established method for managing workout intensity. Zone 2 training, for example, targets 60 to 70 percent of maximum heart rate and is commonly prescribed for fat metabolism and aerobic base building. Zone 4 and 5 training targets near-maximum output for performance improvement. If the heart rate reading on the console is consistently off by 10 to 20 beats per minute, a user training "in Zone 3" may actually be undertraining or overtraining without realizing it.

The lawsuit alleged that Precor's marketing represented the heart rate monitoring capability in a way that implied greater accuracy than the technology actually delivered.

The Broader Issue With Contact-Based Heart Rate Sensors

It is worth understanding the technology at the center of this case. Contact heart rate sensors, the metal pads or grips built into treadmill handlebars, use photoplethysmography or impedance-based electrical detection to estimate heart rate. Both methods are susceptible to signal interference from sweat, grip pressure variation, motion artifact, and skin type. The readings they produce are estimates, not clinical measurements.

Chest strap heart rate monitors, which measure actual electrical signals from the heart muscle, are significantly more accurate. Most modern Precor treadmills also include a chest strap receiver, and that system will produce readings far more reliable than the handlebar sensors. The lawsuit's relevance is that consumers who relied on the handlebar sensors, reasonably assuming they were accurate, may have been training on incorrect data.

What Precor Owners in Dallas Fort Worth Should Know

If you own a Precor treadmill and use heart rate data to guide your training, the most practical takeaway from this case is simple: use a chest strap rather than the handlebar sensors for accurate readings. Polar, Garmin, and Wahoo all make compatible chest straps, and Precor treadmills have long been designed to receive that signal.

For consumers who purchased Precor equipment specifically for heart rate monitoring features and feel the product did not deliver as represented, check the class-action documentation for any eligibility requirements and claim windows that may still be open.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the handlebar heart rate sensors on my Precor treadmill inaccurate?

Contact-based handlebar sensors are inherently less accurate than chest strap monitors due to signal interference from motion, sweat, and grip variation. For casual awareness of approximate heart rate during exercise, they are useful. For precision heart rate zone training, a chest strap is the appropriate tool. Precor treadmills include a chest strap receiver input specifically for this reason.

My Precor treadmill console is not displaying heart rate even when I grip the sensors. Is that a sensor issue or a console issue?

Both are possible. The most common causes are oxidized or dirty sensor contacts on the handlebars, a loose wire connection between the sensors and the console, or a console display issue. In most cases this is a straightforward repair. A technician can diagnose the signal path from sensor to display and identify where the failure is occurring.

How long do Precor treadmills typically last?

Precor builds for commercial durability, and a well-maintained Precor treadmill can realistically run 10 to 15 years or longer in a residential setting. The key is preventive maintenance: belt lubrication, deck inspection, belt tension and alignment checks, and motor brush inspection on older models. We see residential Precor units from the early 2010s still running well after a professional tune-up and belt replacement.
